## The project

A Cyr wheel is a ring apparatus used for movement with a performer inside the ring, typically used in circus type performances.  The inspiration came from seeing one in a performance and then I decided to take some classes to learn how to use one.  They are a little hard to find in the US and can be pricey, so I figured that I could just make my own!

## Design and fabrication

In my mind, the design was pretty straight forward.  Get a pipe, bend it to into a circle, cut it up and do a very similar process for the inner tube that connects the outer tube.  And it would be that easy, if I had anything other than a manual pipe bender.  Turns out bending the aluminum tubing into a perfect circle is not as easy as I thought.  And the inner connecting rods are solid aluminum, which was even harder.  Well after a couple of days of bending the tubing, everything could be cut into 5 pieces.  All the holes were drilled and tapped and the cyr wheel could be easily assembled and disassembled.  

Once this step was completed, the wheel needed to be skinned with a polycarbinate skin to protect the floor it would be on and to add grip.  This might have been the hardest part because the tubing needed to be cut to match the size of each of the 5 pieces. But a special end cap needed to be made to plug the tube on one side with a hole in the middle.  An air compressor was then used to plug the hole .  This allowed the tubing to expand over the aluminum tubing and slide over it completely.

## The finished wheel

The wheel isn't perfectly flat, but its close enough.  Since this is an all aluminum wheel, it is ligher than most wheels.  This makes it slightly more tricky to use since it doesn't carry momentum that well.  All and all, I'm glad I decided to take on the challenge to build this.
